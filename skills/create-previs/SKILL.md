# Pre-vis

A pre-vis mocks up a location's layout: the place built simply in 3D, grey shapes at their true positions and sizes, so a good shot of it can be taken before anything is painted. That one shot is what later images of the place start from: an image model sees a place that makes sense, and paints its own picture of it.

A good pre-vis shot matches the floor plan exactly and reads clearly as that kind of place: the floor, the walls, the openings, and each fixture are told apart by tone, from the camera position that shows the place best. A failed one moves something the plan does not move, or picks a view where nobody can tell what the place is.

Keep to the place. No characters, no lighting, no scene cameras: those come later, from the story.

## Find the specifics

- **The execution skill** that implements `create-previs` in this setup builds and renders the mock-up; when none does, the agent builds it exactly itself, from the plan's numbers. It is never generated by an image model, which cannot keep a plan's measurements.

It comes from the setup, as the [prompt roles](../../docs/prompt-roles.md) describe.

## What it needs

From the caller:

- the approved floor plan, with every position and size in metres;
- the location's research, especially how such a place is best shot, and the photograph of each element the place holds;
- a folder for the mock-up and its shot.

## 1. Build the mock-up

Build every wall with its openings, every fixture, and every light fitting from the plan's numbers. Give each a simple shape that reads as what it is, taken from its photograph in the research: a stool as a round seat on a post, a lamp as a shade on a cord, a booth as a bench, a back, and a table. A box that could be anything is a guess handed to the image model. Compare the finished mock-up against the research photographs, element by element, and add what the place holds but the mock-up lacks. Keep a key that names each element and the photograph it was built from. Colour-code each kind of thing, so the parts are told apart at a glance: the walls and the floor in two plain neutral tones, and each common kind of thing in its own flat colour, such as seating in one colour, counters in another, tables in a third, working equipment in a fourth, windows and doors dark, and light fittings in yellow. Use the same colour for every thing of a kind. Shade each colour lighter on top and darker on the sides turned away. Check it against the plan: every number used, nothing added.

## 2. One good shot

Place one camera where the research says this kind of place is best shown, so the shot shows as much of the place as reads clearly. Render it at the film's aspect ratio. Show it with the camera's position marked on the floor plan, and its reason in a sentence. The user approves it, or asks for the camera moved.

## 3. The views of the place

Once the place's primary canonical location is approved, place a camera square-on to each wall, facing it straight, framed so every object along that wall reads clearly, and a camera for each shot the concept art needs. Render each in grey and show them all, marked on the plan, with how each camera sits relative to the original one. Each becomes a view of the place, made from the primary canonical location.

## 4. A new camera, when asked

When the user wants an approved image of the place seen from another angle, place a further camera on the same mock-up, near enough to the original camera that the place is still recognisable from it, and render it in grey. Show it beside the original shot, both marked on the plan, with how far it moved and turned from the original. The user approves it, or asks for it moved.

## Hand back

- the approved shot of the place, and the same shot in plain greys, which is what an image model receives, so the mock-up's colours do not carry into the painting;
- the camera's position, height, direction, and lens, and its reason;
- what the mock-up was built from, and its key: each element, named, with the photograph it was built from;
- any further camera the user approved, in grey, with its move from the original camera.
