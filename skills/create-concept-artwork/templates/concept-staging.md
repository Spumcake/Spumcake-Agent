## Prompt

The first attached image is a finished concept painting of an empty {{place}}. {{references_list}} Place {{who}} into the first image: {{where_and_what}}. {{scale}} Paint {{each}} in the simplified shapes of {{their_image}}, but with the first image's brush size: the same size of brushstroke across the whole painting, on {{them}} as on {{nearby_objects}}, so {{they}} {{has}} no more detail than anything else in the {{room}}. {{detail_level}}Shade {{them}} as flatly as {{their_image}} does: each part of {{them}}, {{their_parts}}, in one or two large, simple areas of colour, a lit side and a shadow side, and no modelling of muscles, wrinkles, or folds. {{They}} {{is}} painted, not cel-shaded: each area keeps the loose brushstrokes and slight changes of colour of the painting around {{them}}, and the edge between light and shadow is a brushed edge, neither a smooth gradient nor a hard, clean line. Change the first image's light in {{four_ways}} and keep everything else in it as it is, the same painting and the same objects. First, the light comes from {{light_source}}, which is the only {{light}} lit{{lights_off}}. Second, the {{room}}'s values follow depth, distance from the camera, not areas of the frame: the plane of focus is {{subject}}'s distance from the camera, and everything at that depth, across the whole width of the picture, keeps its value; things nearer the camera than {{they}} {{is}}, and things further away, are gradually darker the further their depth is from {{theirs}}. The foreground, {{foreground}}, falls into deep shadow, clearly darker than {{beside_them}}, and the background, {{background}}, is only very slightly darker than the middle of the {{room}}, lit by the ambient light so {{background_details}} read clearly{{background_kept_dark}}. It is not a vignette: nothing is darker for being near the edge of the frame. Every colour and shape stays readable. Third, a soft, {{ambient_colour}} ambient light reaches the rest of the {{room}}, away from {{subject}}, so {{rest_of_place}} each show their own colours, edges, and painted texture rather than sinking into one flat dark; it lifts the {{room}}, not {{them}}: the shadows on {{them}} stay as deep as they are. Every shadow follows the {{light}}, without the {{light}} or the {{room}} getting any brighter: each object is lighter on the side that faces the {{light}} and darker on the side away from it, and casts a clear shadow on the surface beneath it, pointing away from the {{light}}. {{cast_shadows}} {{fourth_light}} Add nothing that no attached image has. The light on {{them}} is rim light from {{the_light}} {{direction}}, not a soft light over {{them}}: thin, bright edges of {{light_colour}} light only along the {{rim_side}} outline of {{their}} form, {{rim_parts}}, each painted as a brushed stroke; the {{light}} also lights {{lit_surface}}. Everything inside those edges, {{inside_parts}}, stays in {{shadow_colour}}, dark but readable{{face_shade}}. Light comes from no other direction: no edge of {{them}} is lit from {{other_directions}}.

## Filling it

Every slot is filled for every image. The examples are from Last order's approved concept artwork (2026-11-12).

- `{{references_list}}`: each other image, named as what it is: "The second attached image is the finished concept painting of the character, a tired cook."
- `{{where_and_what}}`: where the character stands, which way the body faces, what each hand does, named by the character's side and the frame's, where the eyes look, never at the camera, and the expression with its strength: "behind the counter on the right, close to the camera, under the third pendant lamp, the one nearest the camera, his body facing the camera, both hands flat on the counter wiping the counter with the mint towel under his right hand, the hand on the left of the frame, nearer the front door, his head level and his eyes looking down at his hands, not at the camera and not behind him, speaking quietly, his mouth barely open, his face tired and calm; behind him, small and far away, the front door with its glass panel stays dark and empty".
- `{{scale}}`: the character's size against the nearest objects: "He is a big man: the counter top reaches his waist, the third lamp hangs just above his head, and his shoulders are wider than two stools side by side."
- `{{room}}`: the word for the space inside it: "room". `{{the_light}}`: the light by its short name for the rim sentence: "the third lamp".
- `{{detail_level}}`: when the subject's level-of-detail sheet is sent (`create-detail-sheet`), the figure that matches the subject's height in the frame, ending with a space: close when the subject fills most of the frame's height, middle at about half, far at about a quarter, very far at about an eighth; such as "Paint them with the detail of the third figure from the left in the third image, the far one: a few patches of colour, the white hair and suit, a red dot for the bow tie, and two black hands, with no face marks. " The sheet is then named in `{{references_list}}`, such as "The third attached image is a level-of-detail sheet of the same character, painted at four distances from the camera." Last order's approved artwork was made before the sheet existed, and leaves it empty.
- `{{nearby_objects}}`: "the walls, the counter, and the stools". `{{their_parts}}`: "his head, his arms, his shirt, and his apron".
- `{{four_ways}}`: "four ways", or "three ways" when `{{fourth_light}}` is left out.
- `{{light_source}}`: the one light, named so it can be found in the painting: "the third pendant lamp, the one just above him". `{{lights_off}}`: any light the painting shows lit that is now off, such as "; the second lamp, the middle one, is off", or nothing. `{{light}}`: its short name: "lamp".
- `{{subject}}`, `{{theirs}}`: "the cook", "his".
- `{{foreground}}`, `{{background}}`: named by their objects across the whole width of the frame, never as areas of it: "the nearest stools and the booth and table at the lower left"; "the far wall, the clock, and the front door". `{{beside_them}}`: "the counter beside him". `{{background_details}}`: "the wood, the clock's face, and the door's frame". `{{background_kept_dark}}`: what the story keeps dark, such as ", while the door's glass panel stays dark", or nothing.
- `{{ambient_colour}}`: from the style guide's light palette: "cool grey". `{{rest_of_place}}`: "the booths, the windows, the floor, the stools, and the far wall".
- `{{cast_shadows}}`: each object's shadow and where it falls from this light, the character's own included: "The nearest stools cast their shadows on the floor away from the counter, towards the booths; the register, the pie case, and the napkin dispensers cast theirs along the counter, away from him, towards the far end; and he, directly under the lamp, casts his own shadow straight down onto the counter around his hands."
- `{{fourth_light}}`: a second, faint light the place has, or nothing: "Fourth, a subtle, faint moonlight, cool blue-white, comes in through the windows on the left: it lays soft, pale shapes of the window panes on the booth seats, the tables, and the floor beside the windows, and catches the edges of the booths nearest the glass; it is much weaker than the lamp and does not reach him."
- `{{direction}}`: "directly above him". `{{light_colour}}`: "cool grey-white". `{{rim_side}}`: the side of the outline that faces the light: "top" for a light above. `{{rim_parts}}`: the edges on that side: "the crown of his head, the tops of his shoulders, and the top edges of his forearms and hands". `{{lit_surface}}`: "the counter in front of him".
- `{{inside_parts}}`: "his face, his chest, his apron, and his lower body". `{{shadow_colour}}`: "the room's dim charcoal-blue shadow". `{{face_shade}}`: ", his brow shading his eyes", or nothing. `{{other_directions}}`: "the side or from behind" for a light above; "the front or the other side" for a light behind.
