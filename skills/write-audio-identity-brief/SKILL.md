# Audio identity brief

What a film sounds like carries as much of its feeling as what it looks like, often more: a buzzing light, a door's bell, the silence after a line. Before any voice is made or any sound is chosen, the brief says what the sound is for. The voice briefs and the sound design brief answer to it, and the voices, sounds, music, and mix answer to them.

A good brief can be heard in the head: "after the line, the light's buzz dips once and nothing replies" can be checked against a mix; "atmospheric" cannot. A failed brief lists sounds without saying what they do.

This skill makes no sound. It reads the user's brief, writes the audio identity brief as a finished draft, and stops at the user's edits and approval. It does not interview the user.

## The chain

As the user set on 2026-11-12, the film's sound is decided in this order, each document narrowing the one before and never repeating it:

1. the beat sheet, for what happens;
2. **the audio identity brief**, this skill: what the sound is for;
3. **a voice brief per speaking character** (`write-voice-brief`): what the voice sounds like and how each line is delivered;
4. **the sound design brief** (`write-sound-design-brief`): every sound, cue by cue, and the room under them;
5. the skills that make the voices and the sounds from those briefs.

## What it needs

From the caller:

- the concept: the user's brief;
- the visual identity brief, so the sound and the picture serve the same intent;
- the beat sheet, when one exists;
- a folder for the brief.

## 1. Read the brief

Take everything the user's brief, the visual identity brief, and the beat sheet say or imply about sound. Where they are silent, decide from what the story needs, and mark each decision *proposed*. Decide, specifically enough to be heard:

- **The feeling, moment by moment:** for each beat, what the sound makes the audience feel, and which kind of sound does it, such as the room's hum, a single sound the story names, a voice, or silence.
- **Silence:** where it falls, and what it means.
- **At the turn:** what changes in the sound.
- **Music:** whether there is any. If so, what it does, where it enters and leaves, and how present it is; if not, what takes its place.
- **Never:** what the film must never sound like.

How each voice sounds and how each line is delivered belong to the voice briefs; each sound's timing, place, and level to the sound design brief. This brief names them only to say what they are for.

## 2. Write the brief

Fill [the brief template](templates/audio-identity-brief.md). Point to the brief and the beat sheet for what they fix, instead of restating them; keep every statement something a listener could check, and mark everything the user did not say as *proposed*.

## 3. Hand it on

The brief is finished when it is written; the voice briefs and the sound design brief are written from it at once. The user may edit it at any time, directly or by saying what to change; apply every edit, dated, and check what was built on it.

## Hand back

- the audio identity brief;
- the speaking characters, each to get a voice brief.

Run `review-consistency` on the brief before showing it.
